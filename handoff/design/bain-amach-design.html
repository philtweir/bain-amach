Design board · working title

Bain amach

The UI for the Ulster Irish compound-verb card game: a playable table, the card system, and every screen in the flow. Built from the handoff spec; the rules are the simulator's final ruleset.

01 · The core screen

Play the table

A working prototype in turn mode against one bot, with the full rules: the five-pile river, full lines, lines through a particle already down, wildcards, lone verbs, swaps and stalls. Tap rather than drag; everything you can use lights up.

Laying a full line in three taps

  1. Tap a meaning card in your hand. Every verb that can complete one of its three meanings lights up, on the table and in your hand, along with any particle a line could pass through.
  2. Tap a verb. The line appears as a ghost on the table and the river piles it needs start to pulse. A wildcard stands in for a particle the river isn't showing.
  3. Tap “Lay it”, or the ghost itself. “Other spot” cycles through the places the line fits.

Other plays

  • Lone verb: tap a verb with no meaning selected, then “Lay alone”. You draw 2.
  • Swap: select a meaning card, then tap the face-up one beside the river.
  • Draw and pass when nothing fits. Once both players pass in a row, every river pile turns its top card under.
  • Read the table: with nothing selected, tap any card to see its line. Use + and − to zoom; meanings print in full when tiles are large enough.

Design decisions to carry into the build

  • Gorse yellow means “you can use this now”, and nothing else. Bot plays flash in the bot's colour.
  • Particles and wildcards carry an axis stripe along their line. A particle shared by two crossing lines gets a cross stripe and a + badge.
  • Every completed line is announced with the Irish first and the meaning under it.
  • Race mode, the tutorial, pass-and-play hand-over and the recap are drawn as screens in section 03; the build adds them around this same table.
02 · Cards

The card system

Every card is a square, so particles can turn to lie along a row or a column without breaking the grid. In print, verbs and particles carry their word on all four sides so they read from any seat; in the app the word stays upright and the stripe carries the axis.

Three sizes in the app

Reading a dense table

A meaning's line is the particle whose stripe points into it. Here cuir ar runs across, tabhair ar runs down through the same ar, and the shared tile shows both stripes with a +.
